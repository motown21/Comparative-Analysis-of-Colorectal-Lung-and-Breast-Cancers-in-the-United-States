# Comparative Analysis of Colorectal, Lung, and Breast Cancers in the United States
 
## Overview
 
This collaborative data analytics project examined how colorectal, lung, and breast cancers compare in prevalence, risk factors, survival outcomes, and responsiveness to treatment within the United States.
 
The project integrated clinical, genomic, and mortality datasets to evaluate how factors such as age, sex, mutation burden, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and mortality differ across cancer types. Using data analysis and visualization techniques, our team identified patterns that may help improve understanding of cancer progression and treatment responses.

## Research Question
 
How do colorectal, lung, and breast cancers compare in prevalence, risk factors, survival outcomes, and responsiveness to treatment in the United States?

## Project Objectives
 
- Compare prevalence across colorectal, lung, and breast cancers

- Analyze survival outcomes and mortality patterns

- Communicate findings through statistical analysis and data visualization

## Key Findings
 
### Prevalence
 
- Breast cancer was the most prevalent cancer in the clinical dataset.
- Colorectal cancer ranked second.
- Lung cancer had the fewest cases within the study dataset.
 
### Age and Risk Factors
 
- Cancer incidence increased with age.
- More than half of diagnosed patients were over age 50.
- Breast cancer showed an earlier age of onset compared with colorectal and lung cancers.
 
### Survival Outcomes
 
- Lung cancer accounted for the highest proportion of deaths during 2020–2021.
- Colorectal cancer was the second leading cause of mortality among the three cancer types.
- Mortality increased significantly with age across all cancer categories.
 
### Genomic Findings
 
- Colorectal cancer demonstrated the highest mutation counts and greatest genetic variability.
- Lung cancer showed the highest Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB).
- Breast cancer had the lowest mutation burden and TMB values.
 
### Treatment Responsiveness
 
- Higher TMB values are commonly associated with improved responsiveness to immunotherapy.
- Lung cancer demonstrated the highest median TMB, suggesting stronger potential responsiveness to immunotherapy treatments.
